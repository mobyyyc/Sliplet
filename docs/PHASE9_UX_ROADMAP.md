# Phase 9 · Everyday receipt UX

**Proposed outcome.** When a receipt reads correctly, the owner can compare it with the original, confirm it, and put it in the wallet without opening an editor. When one line is wrong, correcting it should be a small detour that returns to the same place. A saved receipt should be easy to find by something the owner remembers buying.

**Starting evidence.** The owner reported a correct Gemini read on the iPhone 15 Pro. The current review view begins with “Check first” and renders purchase line editors inside a Form. Upload preview, local recognition, focused corrections, save-as-draft, wallet motion, item search, splits, Face ID and backup already exist. Phase 8 observed 18–45 second successful provider waits. These observations guide the audit. Current tap counts, correction time and frame performance need a fresh baseline.

Use the existing leather pocket, paper edges and layer shadows as the visual foundation. Reserve Liquid Glass for interactive controls and keep paper readable. Use a reachable primary action and native controls across screens. Prefer a clear reading hierarchy before adding gestures.

Main handles one approved task at a time, records the result and commit, and presents the next task for approval. The [P9-01 audit and screen sketches](design/phase9/UX_AUDIT.md) document the current experience. P9-02 now opens reconciled imports on digital paper. Original preserves reading position, and the independent confirmation stays above the save actions. In the 36-item Simulator fixture, the selected reading position survived comparison within a three-point tolerance and confirmation required zero additional scroll gestures. Incomplete imports and saved-receipt edits continue to open in the editor. P9-03 adds focused correction from purchase and adjustment rows on review paper. A 36-item Simulator walkthrough exercised Cancel and Apply on the same row and checked its return position within three points. A changed amount can leave a visible mismatch for later draft review. P9-04 separates opening the photo, reading on-device and waiting for Gemini. Stopping a read keeps the current photo available. A new Gemini attempt restores its covers and starts at preview with fresh consent. The fictional upload tests cover exact payloads, duplicate taps, cancellation, offline and timeout recovery. P9-05 restores the wallet scroll offset during the return animation. A 30-receipt walkthrough reproduced a roughly 15-point shift after opening the newest paper. After the fix, the same test returned it within two points. The wallet also avoids reordering receipts and measuring every visible paper during elastic scrolling. Archive and Starred are grouped at the top of Settings.

The P9-05 Simulator checks covered sparse and crowded wallets, reversing a revealed action, item search and return, split entry, and restoring a starred receipt after archiving and relaunching. The Simulator performance run returned no numerical hitch measurements. P9-06 is the personal beta and includes checking motion on the iPhone 15 Pro.

**P9-06 personal beta · accepted.** On the iPhone 15 Pro Simulator, the same fictional clean read took four taps after recognition and zero editor visits in both the P9-01 baseline and this build. From the Original tap to the Finish tap, the automated interaction took 7.30 seconds at baseline and 3.24 seconds in this run. These are single-run automation times including UI waits. On the 36-item paper, confirmation was reachable without scrolling after Original or a row correction. The earlier full-form walkthrough took 20 short swipes. Unit and Unit price now each expose a single accessible field label and the same keyboard dismissal control as Quantity. The long-paper check verified both controls, cancellation, applying a correction, scroll restoration, Finish and access to the saved total.

The iPhone 18 Pro Simulator also passed six focused checks covering dark review, accessibility descriptions and hit regions on five screens, large text and Reduce Motion in search, upload controls, clean saving and long receipt bottom clearance. The signed update was installed in place on the owner's iPhone 15 Pro. The final phone adjustment removed the gradient on item text while keeping the wallet rim shadow (a231e93). The owner approved closing Phase 9 on November 2, 2026. Separate results for camera orientation, spoken VoiceOver and physical-device motion were not reported.
